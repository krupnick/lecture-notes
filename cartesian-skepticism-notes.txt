## Our central question

prompt:
On the face of it, we know a lot. 

Someone give me some easy examples of things that you know.

note:
I know that CA is a state; that Sacramento is the capital; that the GSW are a basketbal team; that dinosaurs once existed; that there will be some airplane flights from SFO to Newark later today; and so on.

prompt:
Yet there are also some compelling reasons for saying the opposite---that we know essentially nothing about the world.

What are those reasons?

Well, what if all of this is some grand illusion? How could I ever rule out that possibility? It doesn't seem like I can. But if I'm conceding that this might all be some grand illusion, then I'm thereby conceding that I *don't* know what's really going on in the world.

prompt:
What is going on here? Do we know all sorts of things, or do we know essentially nothing? 

This will be our central question for the next few modules.

Does anyone want to say now where there initial sympathies lie?

note:
Give a couple of students a chance to say which view they think is correct.

## Descartes's Meditation 1 as a locus classicus for skeptical reasoning

prompt:
The default view is that we know a lot. That's the view that takes no special insight or careful thought. It's common sense.

Arriving at the skeptical view takes some amount of persuasion. Someone has to lay out the argument for you. 

So it makes sense to examine the reasons for skepticism. What are they exactly? Do they hold up under scrutiny?

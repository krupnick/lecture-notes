#Pascal's Wager
## PHIL-1 Lecture notes

## What Pascal is arguing for

prompt:
What is Pascal arguing for?

note:
Not trying to prove that God exists. 

Sees believing in God as a *choice*, like the choice of what school to attend.

What's the best choice for you? If you are rational, what should you choose?

Pascal says that *any* rational person would choose to believe in God.

## The basic idea of Pascal's argument

prompt:
What's the basic idea of his argument?

note:
Believing in God gives you the chance at an infinite reward.

To fully assess it, we need to spell it out carefully.

Modern treatments of Pascal's argument see it as relying on "standard decision theory." So to understand his argument, as typically understood, we need to talk about decision theory.

## Background on standard decision theory

prompt:
Suppose I offer you the opportunity to bet on how much rain there will be in New Delhi, India this month.

    | | |
    | Bet | |
    | Don't bet | |

prompt: 
In particular, you have the opportunity to bet that there will be at least 3 inches of rain. 

I tell you that if there is at least 3 inches of rain, then I will give you some money.
    table:
    | | ≥ 3 in. rain |
    | Bet | earn some money |
    | Don't bet | |

prompt:
But if there is less than 3in, then I will take a certain amount of money from you.
    table:
    | | ≥ 3 in. rain | < 3 in. rain
    | Bet | earn some money | lose some money
    | Don't bet | |

prompt:
And of course if you don't bet, then nothing happens.
    table:
    | | ≥ 3 in. rain | < 3 in. rain
    | Bet | earn some money | lose some money
    | Don't bet | status quo | status quo

prompt:
Would you take the bet?

note:
No.

prompt:
Why not?

note:
Not clear how much money is at stake (nor what the odds of that much rain are).

prompt:
What happens if we change the outcome for less than 3 inches of rain to "earn some money"?
    table:
    | | ≥ 3 in. rain | < 3 in. rain
    | Bet | earn some money | earn some money
    | Don't bet | status quo | status quo
Do you take the bet?

note:
Yes. Because now you win either way. No matter what happens, you are better off betting.

prompt:
And what if we change the outcome for less than 3 inches of rain to "status quo"?
    table:
    | | ≥ 3 in. rain | < 3 in. rain
    | Bet | earn some money | status quo
    | Don't bet | status quo | status quo
Do you take the bet?

note:
Yes, because you can't end up worse off by betting, and you could end up better off. 

The decison to bet "dominates" the decision not to bet.
